Day job (the serious part)

I run technology strategy and information security for a global FinTech company. In plain language: I build the rails that keep money moving and the handrails that stop it falling off. It's a job where "boring" is a compliment — the more invisible my work is, the better it's going.

Career moments that sound like a lot until you've actually done them:

  • Built a global engineering org — took a scrappy startup DevOps crew and grew it into a worldwide operation, including standing up an engineering department in Cameroon. (The geography lesson was a bonus.)
  • Certified the company to ISO 27001:2022 — i.e. arranged our affairs so an auditor could find nothing embarrassing. They looked. There was none.
  • Got registered with Indonesian financial regulators — proof that whatever you fear about paperwork, someone in Jakarta has already filed more of it.
  • Led a platform architecture overhaul — the tech version of repainting the Golden Gate Bridge: start at one end, and by the time you finish, it's time to start again.

Education: Master's, University of Ljubljana — plus a standing habit of buying further courses and then having to actually do them (Cambridge Judge CTO program, Henley exec coaching). I keep hoping prestige is contagious. It isn't. You have to build it yourself.

The philosophy, translated

In the corporate version, this is called Leadership Philosophy: The Flow, and it is full of words like "holistic" and "collective intelligence." Here is the honest translation:

Good organizations, like good rivers, are just water flowing downhill without getting stuck. My entire job is removing the boulders — the unclear messages, the silos, the fear — and then getting out of the way and letting capable people work. I borrowed this from the Taoists (flow), the Buddhists (right speech), and roughly every wisdom tradition that noticed life goes better when you stop fighting it.

A leadership philosophy that cites Systems Theory plus Buddhism, Christianity, Hinduism, Islam, Judaism, Confucianism and Taoism. It's entirely possible this is not a philosophy at all and is simply me agreeing with everyone and calling it strategy. You decide.

In summary

I am a systems nerd who likes humans, a technology executive who writes poetry, and a serious professional who will discuss at length the correct e-bike cadence for maximum smugness on a hill. I have standardized the way a whole company talks to itself, and still cannot quite figure out how to talk to myself with the same clarity. That, I suspect, is the real job everyone keeps hiring me for, in disguise.

I keep six tabs of world wisdom traditions open at all times, and my spiritual practice is trying to remember that they all say the same thing.
