Day job (the serious part)

I design information security for a living — currently as an Information Security Architect at AppLovin in Berlin. Before that I was Information Security Officer at auxmoney, and before I worried about other companies' data I was a developer and CTO at Kuelap, where I got to build things instead of just defending them. In plain language: I build the rails that keep systems and money moving, and the handrails that stop them falling off.

Security is a job where "boring" is the highest compliment — the quieter I am, the better it's going. But let's be honest about what it means in practice:

  • I am professionally suspicious of everything. That link, that PDF, that "urgent" request from your CEO — I have made a career out of assuming it's a trap until proven otherwise. I'm sorry in advance for the 2FA.
  • I speak fluent "no." Most of the security job is recommending against fun things, then explaining it wasn't personal. It's always personal. It's just also correct.
  • My earlier CTO days are why I still believe engineering teams are fundamentally good — they just occasionally need somebody to remind them that the internet is populated by other people.

Education: M.A. in Theology from the University of Ljubljana. Yes, the person defending your data studied theology. It turns out "humans are fallen and will disappoint you" is basically the whole threat model.

The philosophy, translated

Somewhere in the corporate-detritus of my career there is a document called Leadership Philosophy: The Flow, full of words like "holistic" and "collective intelligence." Here is the honest translation:

Good organizations, like good rivers, are just water flowing downhill without getting stuck. My entire job is removing the boulders — the unclear messages, the silos, the fear, the unpatched server — and then getting out of the way and letting capable people work. I borrowed some of this from the Taoists (flow), some from the Buddhists (right speech), and most of it from Christianity's ancient, hard-won wisdom about how broken and how redeemable people are, all at once.

A leadership-and-security philosophy that rests on the conviction that systems work when the humans in them are treated like humans. It's possible this is less a philosophy and more me agreeing with everyone and calling it strategy. You decide.

In summary

I am a systems nerd who likes humans, a theology graduate who runs security, and a serious professional who will discuss at length the correct bass setup for maximum doomy tone. I've spent my career holding other people's secrets and my own children's attention, and I still cannot quite figure out how to talk to myself with the clarity I demand from everyone else. That, I suspect, is the real job — in disguise.

I keep six tabs of world wisdom traditions open at all times, and my spiritual practice is trying to remember that they all say the same thing.
